
1. **Never address or describe the protagonist directly** - No "you," "your," "Lukas," or protagonist descriptions. The camera should feel *behind* protagonist's eyes, not watching them.

2. **Establish tone through sensory environment** - Setting details should telegraph genre. Erotica in upscale boutique = describe silk textures, the way light catches on skin, body language that lingers. Horror = oppressive atmosphere, uncanny details.

3. **Use character descriptions as setting rules reference** - If a character card establishes "Free Use World" mechanics, show those rules naturally through environment and NPC behavior. If it's Worm, show power dynamics and costume culture. Let the world-building emerge from how characters exist in space.

4. **Match prose density to genre** - Erotica = lush, sensory, body-focused. Action = tight, kinetic. Mystery = observational, detail-oriented. Horror = atmospheric, building dread.

5. **Ground the supernatural/altered reality naturally** - Show don't tell. Altered physics, social norms, or power dynamics should feel embedded in how the scene *is*, not explained.

6. **End on a pause, not a prompt** - Scene should stop at natural moment. No "What do you do?" if the situation already invites action.

**Core principle:** The opening establishes **where we are, what genre we're in, and what rules apply**—then gets out of the way.
